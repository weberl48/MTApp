1. Welcome

What MCA Manager is, and what your part in it looks like.

MCA Manager is May Creative Arts' practice management app. It's where therapy sessions get logged, clients get managed, invoices get created and sent, and contractors get tracked — all in one place, on your computer or your phone.

As an admin, you're the hub of the daily workflow. Contractors log their sessions; you review and approve them; the app turns approved work into invoices; you send those invoices and record payments. This guide walks you through each of those pieces in the order you'll meet them.

How to use this guide: read sections 1–5 before your first day if you can — they cover everything you'll touch in a normal day. Sections 6–9 are there when you need them. Section 13 is a one-page cheat sheet worth printing.

Start with the walkthroughs, not this document. MCA Manager has built-in interactive walkthroughs that highlight each button on the real screens as you click through. They will orient you far faster than reading — take the App Overview tour first, then come back here. Section 10 lists them in the order to take them.
Before you start: Section 2 explains that clients are not receiving any email yet. Read it before you send your first invoice.

2. Phase 1 — What's Live Right Now

Read this one first. It explains the single most important thing about this stage.

Clients are not receiving anything yet. Every invoice, reminder and portal link the app sends during Phase 1 is redirected to three internal inboxes — Lucas, Amara and you. Nothing reaches a real client until we deliberately switch that off.

We're turning the app on in stages. Phase 1 is you using it for real — logging sessions, reviewing them, creating and sending invoices — while the app's outbound email is held back. That way we find the problems before a client ever sees one.

How you'll know it's on

An amber bar sits across the top of every screen while Phase 1 is active. It lists exactly which inboxes the app is sending to:

If that bar is gone, the app is live and invoices really are going to clients. Nobody will turn it off without telling you first.

What the redirected emails look like

When you send an invoice, it lands in all three inboxes instead of the client's. You'll spot them immediately:

  • The subject line starts with [PILOT → client@example.com] — naming the person who would have received it.
  • A yellow band across the top of the email repeats that warning.
  • Everything else — the wording, the amount, the PDF attachment — is exactly what the client would have got.

What we need from you

Use the app exactly as if it were real. Don't soften anything or skip steps to be safe — the whole point is to exercise the real workflow. When an invoice arrives in your inbox, open it and check:

  • Right client name, spelled correctly
  • Right amount, right session date, right service
  • The PDF opens and reads the way you'd want a client to see it
  • The wording sounds like May Creative Arts

Anything that looks off — however small — is worth reporting. Wording and formatting count. See Section 11.

One thing that is genuinely different: because the client never receives the email, you can't test whether they can pay it. Payment collection is a later phase. For now we're testing that the right invoice, for the right amount, gets generated and sent.

3. Day One Checklist

Six steps, about 20 minutes, and you're fully set up.

  1. Sign in and change your password. Your account is already set up — you don't need to accept an invite. The app lives at https://maycreativearts.vercel.app — bookmark it. Sign in with the temporary password Lucas gives you, then change it immediately under Settings > Profile & Security. If the temporary password doesn't work, use Forgot password? on the login page.
  2. Log in and look around. After logging in you land on the Dashboard. Nothing you click while browsing will send anything to a client — invoices only go out when you explicitly send them.
  3. Set up two-factor authentication. This is required, not optional — the app holds client health information, so May Creative Arts has two-factor turned on for everyone. Have your phone with you when you first log in. The app will walk you through pairing an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy, or whatever your phone already uses), and from then on you'll enter a 6-digit code at login. Save the backup codes it gives you somewhere safe — without them, a lost phone means Lucas has to reset your account manually.
  4. Install the app on your phone. There's no app store download — you install it straight from the browser. On iPhone: open the site in Safari → Share button → Add to Home Screen. On Android: open it in Chrome → tap the Install banner (or menu → Add to Home Screen). On desktop: look for the install icon in Chrome/Edge's address bar.
  5. Take the "App Overview" walkthrough. Open Help from the sidebar and start the App Overview walkthrough. It points out each area of the app on the live screens — the fastest way to get oriented.
  6. Meet the AI helper. The round sparkle button in the bottom-right corner answers questions in plain English — "how do scholarship invoices work?", "what's our no-show fee?". One rule: never type client names or health details into it (it doesn't need them and can't see client data anyway).

4. Finding Your Way Around

The Dashboard

The stats across the top give you the pulse: sessions this month, total clients, recently approved sessions, and pending invoices with the dollars outstanding. 5. Sessions — Your Daily Core

Sessions are the heartbeat of the app. Everything else — invoices, pay, reports — flows from them.

How a session moves through the app

Draft→ Submitted→ Approved

A contractor logs a session and submits it. Submitting is the moment the money side starts: the app immediately creates a pending invoice for each attending client (scholarship and monthly-billed clients are the exception — their sessions accumulate for a monthly batch instead). The session then waits in your review queue until you approve it.

Two side paths: a session can be marked No-Show (client didn't attend — a flat no-show fee is charged, and the contractor still gets their normal session pay) or Cancelled (removed from billing entirely — no invoice).

The Sessions page

Each card shows the date, service type, client, contractor, duration, and a status badge. Use the search bar (client, contractor, or service name) and the filters (status, date range, contractor) to find anything. Sessions with a Submitted badge are waiting on you.

Reviewing and approving

Submitted sessions show Approve and Revise buttons right on the card. Before approving, give it a quick sanity check: right client, right service type, right duration, notes present if required. Then:

  • Approve — confirms the session. Its invoice already exists (created at submission) and is now ready to send whenever you're ready.
  • Revise — sends it back to the contractor with a note explaining what to fix. The pending invoice is removed automatically, and recreated when they resubmit — nothing goes unbilled and nothing gets double-billed.
  • Mark No-Show / Cancel / Delete — available from the session's detail page.

Bulk approve: when several sessions are ready at once (say, end of the week), use the checkboxes — or "Select all submitted" — and hit Approve (N) in the bar that appears. It runs the same checks as approving one at a time.

Logging a session yourself

Mostly contractors log their own sessions, but you can log one for anyone. Click New Session on the Sessions page (on mobile, the floating + button). The form asks for date and time, duration, service type, client(s), and notes. The service type drives all the pricing automatically — you never calculate anything by hand.

Two kinds of notes, and the difference matters: Internal Notes are visible only to staff. Client Notes appear in the client's portal. Both are encrypted for HIPAA compliance.

Tip: the form remembers your last-used time, duration, and service type, and a Log Another button appears after each submission — logging a full day of sessions takes a couple of minutes.

6. Clients

Every client's contact info, billing setup, and history in one place.

The client list

The Clients page shows everyone with their contact info and payment method. The stats at the top flag clients missing contact details. Click any row to open the client's full record: session history, invoices, goals, and shared resources.

Adding a client — the two settings that matter

Click Add Client, enter their name and contact info, and set two fields that drive everything billing-related later:

Question it answersOptions
Payment Method Who pays, and how is the session classified? Determines how much is billed — scholarship clients get the flat scholarship rate and monthly batching. Private Pay · Self-Directed · Group Home · Scholarship · Venmo
Billing Method How does their invoice get delivered? Determines whether you send a Square payment link, an emailed PDF, or track a check by hand. Square · Email · Check · Other
Worth double-checking: a client's payment method is the single most consequential setting in the app — it decides whether they're invoiced per-session or batched monthly. If a new client's billing looks wrong later, this is the first place to look.

The client portal invite

From a client's page you can send a portal invite — a secure link where the client (or their family) sees their session history, notes you've shared, goals, and resources. More in Section 9.

7. Invoices

The app writes the invoices; you decide when they go out.

Where invoices come from

You almost never create an invoice by hand. When a session is submitted, the app generates one pending invoice per attending client, priced from the service type. Pending invoices are drafts — nothing reaches a client until you send it.

The Invoices page

Summary cards up top show pending, awaiting-payment, and overdue totals. The tabs split invoices by status — Pending, Sent, Paid, Overdue — plus filters for Self-Directed and Group Home clients, and the purple Scholarship tab.

Sending an invoice

Open the … menu on any invoice:

  • Send via Email — generates a PDF and emails it to the client.
  • Send via Square — creates a Square invoice with an online payment link. When the client pays online, the invoice marks itself Paid automatically. This is the lowest-effort path for clients who pay by card.

For invoices handled outside the app (checks, Venmo), use the quick Send and Paid buttons to record the status yourself. Checkboxes + the action bar let you Mark Sent / Mark Paid in bulk.

Reminders are automatic. Unpaid sent invoices get email reminders before their due date (by default 7 days and 1 day before, each sent once). You don't chase these by hand.

The Scholarship tab — once a month

Scholarship and monthly-billed clients don't get per-session invoices. Their sessions pile up here, grouped by client and month. At month end, click Generate Invoice on a group — or Generate All — and the app creates one batch invoice per client listing every session as a line item. Batches are created as Pending, so you review before sending like any other invoice.

8. Team

See the whole team, and bring new people on board.

The Team page lists every member with their role and session counts. As an admin you can view the team and invite new members: click Invite Team Member, pick the role (Contractor or Admin), and either email the invite or copy a link to share directly. They set up their own account from the link — the same flow you went through on day one.

Changing roles, pay rates, and removing members is handled by the owner.

9. The Client Portal

What clients see when you send them a portal invite.

The portal is a simplified, secure view for clients and families — no account or password needed, just the tokenized link from their invite. They can see their session history with any Client Notes you or contractors shared, their goals and progress, and resources (files) shared with them. If enabled, they can also request a session, which comes to staff for approval.

Portal links expire after a while (90 days by default) — if a client says their link stopped working, just send a fresh invite from their client page.

Remember the notes rule: anything typed into Client Notes is visible in the portal. Anything for staff eyes only belongs in Internal Notes.

11. Found a Bug? Here's How to Tell Us

You're the first person really using this. What you notice is the whole point of Phase 1.

Report anything that looks wrong, reads badly, or just feels clumsy. You do not need to be sure it's a bug — "this seemed weird" is a perfectly good report. A confusing button is as worth reporting as a broken one, because a client or contractor will hit the same confusion.

Where to send it: email lucasemersonweber@gmail.com. If something is blocking you from working, text instead of emailing so it gets seen straight away.

The recipe: a screenshot, then bullets

  1. Take a screenshot. Capture the whole window if you can, not just the problem area — the surrounding screen tells us which page you were on and what state it was in.
    • Windows: press Windows + Shift + S, drag a box, then paste into the email with Ctrl + V.
    • Mac: press Cmd + Shift + 4, drag a box. It saves to your desktop.
    • iPhone: press the side button and volume-up together.
    • Android: press power and volume-down together.
  2. Write what's wrong as bullets. Short bullets beat a paragraph. Cover these four things:
    • What I was doing — the steps you took, in order
    • What I expected — what you thought would happen
    • What actually happened — what you saw instead
    • Why it matters — what it would cost, confuse or get wrong if a client or contractor hit this
  3. Add the boring details. Date and rough time, whether you were on your phone or a computer, and which client, session or invoice number was involved. These are what let us find it in the logs.

What a strong report looks like

Strong — we can act on this Weak — we'll have to come back and ask

[screenshot attached]

  • What I was doing: Invoices page, opened invoice #1043 for the Miller group session on Jul 29, clicked Send.
  • Expected: the invoice to email out and the status to flip to Sent.
  • Actually happened: a red "Failed to send email" toast, status stayed Pending. Tried twice, same thing.
  • Why it matters: it's a group session so it's our biggest invoice this month, and I can't tell whether it half-sent.
  • Details: Jul 31 around 2:15pm, on my laptop in Chrome.

"The invoices page is broken, it won't let me send anything."

No screenshot, no invoice number, no time. We can't reproduce it, so the first thing that happens is three emails back and forth before anyone can start looking.

Wording and layout count too

Not everything is a crash. These are all worth a report:

  • A label that uses the wrong word for how May Creative Arts actually talks
  • An email to a client that sounds too stiff, too casual, or just wrong
  • Something that takes six clicks that ought to take two
  • A screen that's unreadable or cut off on your phone
  • A number that's right but formatted confusingly

For these, "what's wrong and why" is still the format — tell us what it says now, what it should say, and what a reader would misunderstand.

Check this first: a fair number of "missing" things are deliberate. Contractor pay, rates, analytics, payroll and Settings are hidden from admins on purpose — see Section 12. If something's missing and it's on that list, it's working as intended. If it's missing and it isn't on that list, definitely report it.
A note on client privacy: screenshots may show client names and session notes. When you report something, a first name, initials, or the invoice number is plenty — there's no need to include health details, and please don't forward these screenshots any further than the address above.
